This is due to fewer middle and secondary school places for girls and high population growth rate which puts burden on resources and any investment made is nullified with greater demand. This gap is particularly pronounced at the middle and high school levels. New MICS 2017–18 data reveals that primary NAR in Punjab (65.4 percent) declines sharply at the lower secondary level (36.7 percent) and falls further at the upper secondary level (28.9 percent). Girls from the province’s poorest households are clearly greatly disadvantaged, as demonstrated by their extremely low NAR at the lower secondary (10.2 percent) and upper secondary levels (5.5 percent).
