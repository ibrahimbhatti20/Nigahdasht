In Sindh, total of 9,742 doctors, including medical officers from district level healthcare faculties, private family physicians and paediatricians were trained on use of updated commodities. Out of these, 8, 102 were also oriented on the use of revised DHIS tools for recording and reporting of information. Total of 22,700 Lady Health Workers (LHWs) were covered in these trainings from all 29 districts, covering ~100% of LHWs strength of Sindh province. In Punjab, in addition to doctors, LHWs and LHSs, Lady Health Visitors were also trained on use of updated commodities and MIS. UNICEF, starting from the inception phase, ensured the participation of government stakeholders for enhanced communication thereby helping in creating, storing, transferring and application of the knowledge to these provinces and regions. In this regard, the project engaged policy makers and planners from remaining two provinces (Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa) and three regions (Gilgit Baltistan, Azad Jammu & Kashmir and the Federally Administered Tribal Areas – now merged districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa) and made them an essential part of National RMNCAH&N TWG. Building on the decisions taken during these meetings, the remaining provinces and regions has taken certain steps towards updating their existing systems, like inclusion of dispersible tablets in ‘very essential list of medicines’ of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
