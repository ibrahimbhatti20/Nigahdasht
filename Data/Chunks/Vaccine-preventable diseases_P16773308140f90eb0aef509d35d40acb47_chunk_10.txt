In Punjab, there were proportionally more fully immunized children in rural areas than in urban areas (see figure 7). Focusing exclusively on rural areas, the data show that roughly one-third of the children were vaccinated in Balochistan, a little more than half in Sindh, and 67% in KP. This clearly indicates the need for ramping up efforts to vaccinate children in the rural areas. Gaps in immunization coverage Although immunization coverage has improved over time, disparities exist in terms of coverage by vaccine type, socioeconomic background, and demographics. There is considerable variation in coverage of each type of vaccine among the provinces. Punjab documented the highest coverage for every vaccine while Balochistan has lagged behind (see Table 2). Sindh lags considerably behind Punjab; for some vaccines, such as against polio and measles, the immunization rate in Sindh is lower than in KP. 9 As expected, a household's wealth has a bearing on the likelihood of its children being vaccinated. The two lowest income quintiles lag significantly compared to the richer quintile for all provinces, except Punjab, where coverage is comparable across the income groups (see Figure 8). For Sindh, the difference between the richest quintile and the four lower income quintiles is stark—immunization coverage among the richest was 20% more than in the middle-income quintile and 37% greater than in the poorest quintile. Intriguingly, the immunization coverage among the richest quintile in Sindh was lower than in KP. For Balochistan, the three richest quintiles had similar immunization coverage levels.
