UNICEF is prioritising newborn survival and immunization in 2018-2022 to ensure that every child survives and thrives. Newborn mortality The national neonatal mortality rate in Pakistan is 42 per 1,000 live births, while the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) aim for 12 per 1,000 live births.1 Newborn girls and boys continue to die of conditions that could be managed within households, such as low birth weight, hypothermia and sepsis, as well as from conditions that require interventions in health facilities such as birth asphyxia and prematurity. Neonatal survival is further complicated by poorly performing cross-sectoral family care practices and interventions, including sub-optimal breastfeeding, hygiene and sanitation. Only 69 per cent of births are attended by skilled birth attendants, and the rate of exclusive breastfeeding for children younger than six months is low — only 38 per cent. The limited access to life-saving interventions is further complicated by huge disparities between provinces, rural and urban locations, income, and levels of education, particularly of mothers. In December 2018, Pakistan’s National Assembly passed a resolution in which it recognized the rights of every child born in Pakistan as well as mothers.² In particular, the Assembly resolved that “the State must ensure the provision of the best available early care and safe environment for all children.
