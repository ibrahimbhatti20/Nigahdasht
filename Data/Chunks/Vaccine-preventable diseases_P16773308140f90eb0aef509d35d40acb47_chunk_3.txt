Background Pakistan is the fifth most populous country in the world, with a projected population of 232 million in 2021, of whom 51 million are women of childbearing age and 33 million children under five years of age. The gross national income per capita was US$1,410 in 2019. Periodic macroeconomic crises and a low human capital basis have constrained the country's growth prospects. Between fiscal years (FY) 2019 and 2020, annual economic growth, measured using gross domestic product (GDP), averaged around 4.3% in Pakistan, below the South Asian annual average of 6.2%. According to the World Bank Human Capital Index, if no improvements in health and education service delivery take place, a Pakistani child born today can expect to be only 40% as productive as s/he otherwise could be by age 18. Pakistan has witnessed some improvements in health, nutrition, and population (HNP) outcomes over the years; significant challenges however remain, especially given the progress made by countries of comparable wealth. Pakistan was not able to achieve any of the 2015 HNP-related millennium development goals; and, at current trends, is unlikely to meet most of the HNP-related Sustainable Development Goals by 2030. Goal 3 of the SDGs is to: “ensure healthy lives and promote wellbeing for all at all ages”. Two of the targets of this goal are, to end preventable deaths of newborns and children under five years of age, with all countries aiming to reduce neonatal mortality—death within the first 28 days of birth—to no more than 12 per 1,000 live births, and under-five mortality (the probability of a child dying before their fifth birthday) to no more than 25 per 1,000 live births by 2030. Pakistan reduced its neonatal mortality rate from 53.3 per 1,000 live births in 1990 to 41 deaths per 1,000 live births in 2019, but this rate was still the second highest in the world. Similarly, the under-five child mortality rate ) declined from 120.4 per 1,000 live births in 1990 to 74 per 1,000 live births in 2019. Childhood immunization against vaccine preventable diseases (VPD) presents a highly cost-effective intervention by delivering significant reductions in morbidity and mortality from inexpensive and standardized interventions.
