It is important to note that immunization service delivery in 2020 was hampered due to the outbreak of novel coronavirus disease (COVID-19) and, therefore, the immunization coverage rates were lower than planned. Greater immunization coverage over the period meant that Pakistan was able to reduce the incidence of two VPDs—diphtheria and measles—by 94% and measles by 88% respectively between 1990 and 2020 (see Figures 2 and 3). Diphtheria is a serious infection that causes difficulty in breathing, heart failure, paralysis, and even death especially among children. Measles is a highly contagious disease and can be fatal for ... children under five years of age, the elderly, pregnant women, and those with a weakened immune system. ... Both these diseases can be prevented through vaccination. Pakistan also reduced the incidence of poliomyelitis (a disabling, sometimes fatal disease) between 1990 and 2020 from 777 to 135 cases. Despite this progress, Pakistan is one of the two countries in the world where poliomyelitis is still endemic. According to WHO, eradication of poliomyelitis requires high immunization coverage to block transmission of the virus that is extremely contagious. Several factors explain why some children are still not being vaccinated.
