Introduction 1.1 Air Pollution in Pakistan Air pollution has become a serious concern for the health and well-being of the citizens of Pakistan. As reported by the World Health Organization (WHO) Air Quality Database1, concentrations of fine particulate matter (PM2.5), which is the most damaging to human health, exceeded WHO ambient air quality guidelines for the protection of human health in 2022. In 2019, ambient PM2.5 concentrations in Lahore were as high as 123 µg m-3, which is at least twenty-four times higher than the WHO’s Air Quality Guideline. The Air Quality Index (AQI) values collected from the AirNow data shows that during the winter months, many cities have an AQI of above 150 with Lahore often exceeding 400. Other cities, including Faisalabad, Islamabad, Karachi, Peshawar and Rawalpindi,have air qualities that are largely comparable.
