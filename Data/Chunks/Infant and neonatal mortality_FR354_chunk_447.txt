Design weights were adjusted for cluster level non-response, household level non-response, and for individual non-response to get the sampling weights for women’s and men’s surveys respectively. The differences of the household sampling weights and the individual sampling weights are introduced by individual nonresponse. The final sampling weights were normalised in order to get the total number of unweighted cases equal to the total number of weighted cases at national level, for Appendix A • 353 both household weights and individual weights, respectively. There are four sets of weights to be calculated: ▪ one set for all households selected for the survey ▪ one set for women selected for individual survey ▪ one set for households selected for the male survey ▪ one set for the male individual survey ▪ one set for the domestic violence survey It is important to note that the normalised weights are relative weights, which are valid for estimating means, proportions and ratios, but not valid for estimating population totals nor for pooled data. Also the number of weighted cases by using the normalised weight has no direct relation with the survey precision because it is relative, especially for oversampled areas. The number of weighted cases is much smaller than the number of unweighted cases; the latter is directly related to survey precision. Sampling errors were calculated for selected indicators for the national sample, for the urban and rural areas separately, and for each of the 13 survey domains.
