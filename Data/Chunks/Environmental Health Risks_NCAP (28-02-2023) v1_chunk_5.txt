According to the Global Burden of Disease, in 2019, 235,000 premature deaths were attributable to air pollution exposure in Pakistan3.. Furthermore, smog has led to a decrease in crop yields, presenting challenges to food security and affecting ecosystem production. Several other studies and reports conclude that Pakistan is already facing severe economic and health impacts resulting from air pollution. A recent report from World Bank (WB) notes that air pollution could impose a loss of 6.5 percent of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) per year4. Pakistan faces frequent smog episodes in winter, which are linked to high pollutant concentrations, resulting in severe impact on environment and public health. Punjab experiences periods of low visibility due to fog, mist and smog between November and February each year lasting an average of 10 to 25 days. This is usually a regional event, covering vast expanse from New Delhi to Faisalabad and beyond. Mitigation measures that improve air quality can also reduce GHG emissions that contribute to climate change, meaning that improving air quality can also support achievement of Pakistan’s international commitment to mitigate climate change. 1.2 Impacts of Air Pollution in Pakistan Poor air quality has significant environment, economic/ development and health in Pakistan. A brief account of these on key sectors is presented below: 1.2.1 Health Impacts Outdoor air pollution can cause a range of health problems, including Acute Respiratory Infection (ARI), heart disease, lung cancer, chronic bronchitis, and others. Indoor air pollution can cause similar health problems, including ARI, chronic bronchitis, cataracts, tuberculosis, low birth weight, and others. High PM2.5 concentrations are linked to respiratory and heart diseases such as ischemic heart disease and can lead to an increased death rate. It is estimated that 114,000 deaths that had occurred in 2019 in Pakistan could be attributed to PM2.55, reducing the average life expectancy by 2.7 years.
