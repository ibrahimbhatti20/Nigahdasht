Similarly, children from the wealthiest families are 1.5 times as likely to receive ORS to treat their diarrhoea as the poorest children (Figure 18). There has also been little or no progress in increasing the use of ORS among children with diarrhoea since 2000. This is true for every region with data, including Africa and South Asia, the regions with the greatest diarrhoea burdens (Figure 19). The new ORS formula (known as low-osmolarity ORS) is the ‘gold standard’ for treating childhood diarrhoea, as well as treating dehydration once it occurs. However, data on the use of low-osmolarity ORS speciﬁcally are not available through household surveys.
