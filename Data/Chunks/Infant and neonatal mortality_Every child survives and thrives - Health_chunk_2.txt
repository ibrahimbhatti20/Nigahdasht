Strengthened links between routine immunisation and polio Links between routine immunization and polio have been strengthened under a federal taskforce, multiplying benefits for children. In 2018, UNICEF supported nearly 19,000 polio community health workers in the 11 districts with the lowest immunization coverage in 2018. They devoted a quarter of their time to mobilizing communities for routine immunization, identifying and referring children with no vaccinations against any disease. For example, polio workers supported Pakistan’s October 2018 national measles campaign. During the five annual polio immunization campaigns, over 260,000 volunteer social mobilizers identified non-immunized children and referred them for routine vaccination. However, immense variations in successful referrals were seen, from 99 per cent in the Punjab province to as low as 4-16 per cent in Sindh where coordination between polio and EPI teams was weak in comparison. Nevertheless, innovations in immunization are collectively increasing routine immunization coverage, offering great promise as service delivery is strengthened. EVERY CHILD SURVIVES For every 1,000 babies that are born alive, 42 die as newborns and 62 die before age 1. Today, 74 in every 1,000 Pakistani children born alive die before their 5th birthday.
