Executive Summary Pakistan1 ranks in the countries with high infant mortality rates. As per the available statistics, each year around 89 children under the age of 5 die in 1,000 livebirths. While there are different causes of high mortality, the two most noticeable reasons are acute respiratory infection (pneumonia) and diarrhoea. It was reported2 in 2012-13 that 91,000 children died from pneumonia and 53,300 children died from diarrhoea. As per the Pakistan Demographic and Health Survey (PDHS) of 2012-13, around 23% of children under the age of 5 had diarrhoea two weeks before the survey, 61% were taken to appropriate health provider, only 38% were provided with an oral rehydration salts (ORS), and less than 2% were provided with zinc supplements. Similarly, 16% of children under the age of 5 had acute respiratory infection (ARI) / pneumonia two weeks before the survey, and 42% with ARI received antibiotics. This study estimates the funding required to reduce child mortality and morbidity caused due to diarrhoea and pneumonia. Study objectives and the methodology adopted is provided in Chapter 2. Specific study findings are provided in Chapter 3 while recommendations are provided in Chapter 3.8.
