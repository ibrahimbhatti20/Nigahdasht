Among these prescriptions, 40.0% were girls and 60.0% were boys, which was 45.2% girls and 54.8% boys at the time of mid-term evaluation. Among prescriptions of U5 Pneumonia cases, nearly half of the cases were not classified as per IMNCI guidelines and situation was better in Sindh (73.5% cases of U5 Pneumonia classified) as compared to Punjab (only 7.5% of the U5 Pneumonia cases classified). Findings of the assessment revealed that Amoxicillin was prescribed in any formulation in 71.6% of the prescriptions, 89.6% in Punjab and 63.9% in Sindh. However, Amox DT was prescribed in only 30.2% of the prescriptions. While there is marked improvement in prescription of some form of antibiotic, the prescribing behavior at public facilities were influenced with the type of formulation available at the health facilities. As seen in Punjab where majority of the facilities had availability of syrups, Amox DT was prescribed in only 3.3% of the reviewed U5 Pneumonia prescriptions. In addition to Amoxicillin and Bronchodilators, 93.2% of the U5 Pneumonia prescriptions had other medications, which included antibiotics, anti-pyretic medicines and steam inhalation.
