Similarly, the mean ideal number of children is the same among currently married and ever-married men (4.3). In general, ideal number of children increases considerably with increasing numbers of living children among both women and men (Figure 6.3). For example, ever-married women who have fewer than three living children consider 3.5-3.6 children to be ideal, whereas those with six or more children consider 5.3 children as ideal; similarly, the ideal number of children rises from 3.8-4.0 among men with fewer than three living children to 5.7 among those with six or more children. Forty-nine percent of women believe that their husband would like to have the same number of children they want while 30% believe their husband would like more children. (Table 6.5). Trends: Mean ideal number of children among women has decreased modestly over time, from 4.1 in 1990-91 and 2012-13 to 3.9 in 2017-18. Among men, mean ideal number of children has not changed since 2012-13 (4.3 in both surveys).
